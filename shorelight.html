Overview

As part of a Directed Research Group (DRG) at the University of Washington, I designed and developed a 2D narrative simulation game in GameMaker focused on shoreline sustainability and marine life restoration. Players restore a forgotten coast through tide-based gameplay cycles that shift between exploration, cleanup, and environmental care. The game combines environmental storytelling with soft pixel art to promote awareness of ocean pollution and our connection to coastal ecosystems.

Story Design

A shoreline has been forgotten, littered, and disconnected from its natural rhythm. The game follows the player's journey to clean and restore the coast with the guidance of ocean spirits and the rhythm of the tides. Gameplay aims to shift with the real-time tidal cycle—during low tide, players explore seabeds, clear debris, and rescue marine life; during high tide, they tend floating gardens, solve puzzles, and interact with spirits. As the world gradually revives, players unlock new coastal biomes such as coral reefs, tidepools, and seagrass meadows. The narrative and mechanics work together to reinforce the core objective: restoring the shoreline's natural balance and reconnecting with the environment through mindful, exploratory play.

Core Objective/Golden Theme:
- Restore the shoreline/tidepools and natural rhythm of the environment
- Bring awareness to the importance of ocean sustainability
