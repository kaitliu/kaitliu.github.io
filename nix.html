Concept

Quit smarter, not harder

NIX is a smart e-cigarette device that programmatically reduces nicotine concentration per session over time. As usage continues, the device delivers progressively lower doses, allowing the body to gradually lose its chemical dependency. Eventually, only the behavioral habit remains; and without the chemical reinforcement, that habit is significantly easier to break.

NIX pairs with a companion mobile app that tracks usage, visualizes progress over time, and lets users customize their reduction schedule. Together, the device and app are designed to make the quitting process feel like forward momentum rather than deprivation.

We chose five prototyping methods to cover two distinct dimensions of the product: the physical device and the companion app. Each method was chosen to answer a different set of questions, at a fidelity appropriate to what we needed to learn.

Prototype Evaluation

Our evaluation focused on two themes: usability (whether the interaction was intuitive) and desirability (whether the concept appealed and motivated continued use).

Different dimensions were tested at different fidelities — lower fidelity for usability early on, higher fidelity for desirability later.

Implementation

The NIX prototype was implemented across two parallel tracks: a physical vape prototype and a digital interface. Together they represent a mid to high fidelity prototype.

Digital App Prototype

The figma prototype covers the full user journey from onboarding to ongoing usage.

  • Onboarding screens: Collects nicotine product type, usage frequency, duration, and previous quit attempts. Presents three plan options — AI-powered standard plan, setting a target date, and go at my own pace.
  • Device pairing: Bluetooth connection screen that prompts users to hold the NIX device nearby and detects the device.
  • Home dashboard: Displays a Goal Streak counter, a projected vs. actual nicotine reduction graph, and a color coded calendar showing daily pickup habits.
  • Device panel: Shows NIX pod and nicotine percentage remaining along with estimated quit date and reduction milestones.
  • My activities tab: Provides a detailed view of usage history and scheduled reduction over time.

Physical Device Prototype

The physical NIX device was prototyped in three stages of increasing fidelity.

  • Paper sketch: Annotated hand-drawn diagrams exploring internal hardware layout and shape/structure of vape.
  • Cardboard model: Chipboard mockup of the vape to determine general shape and size.
  • Two 3D printed models: Black filament printed vape with NIX logo on the body. There is a display interface that shows the battery life left of the vape as well as the amount of nicotine and NIX pod fluid is left. The inside of the vape has two compartments for the nicotine side and our NIX pod.

User Testing

We tested our prototypes on a variety of users and attempted to test both the digital and physical aspects of the design concurrently. Our users were people who has experience using some type of smoking device, irrespective of the specific substance. Our users lay within the 20-23 age range and we had a variety of design/non-design students.

Low-fidelity cardboard device

  • Feels like a "real vape" making it inconspicuous
  • User was unsure as to how the internal mechanics would work
  • Wanted to make sure it would be refillable with juice not pods

Low Fidelity 3D Print

  • Felt too small to be usable
  • Users were still unable to feel like this was a real product and therefore we were unable to receive useful behavioral feedback from this prototype.

Mid Fidelity 3D Print

  • User tended to pick up the device by the mid-body, this leaves room for the button to accidentally be pressed.
  • User suggested that this is a useful product but is too thick for easy use.
  • User also said he'd pay $40-$50 for this device

Low-fidelity Figma App

  • Logging a new session was a little confusing
  • Generally able to understand the main uses of the app
    • Preferred positive reinforcement

  • User liked the app and found it informative but found the projected nicotine use to be confusing

Analysis

Physical Prototype Results Summary

Three key findings from the first iteration evaluation directly informed the NIX design:

Findings Design Solution
Chemical Dependency: Users generally struggled to self-regulate reduction. NIX auto adjusts the nicotine output level if user surpasses their scheduled weaning goal.
Behavioral Habits: Users were typically unaware of how frequently they picked up their device each day. NIX tracks pickup frequency and visualizes it in a color coded calendar, surfacing unconscious habit patterns.
Streak Based Motivation: We found that positive reinforcement worked best as users found streak tracking more motivating than other goal-completion methods tested. NIX displays a streak counter on the top of the home screen linked directly to the user's quit goal.

Digital Prototype Results Summary

Theme Insights

Engagement Style

Users preferred passive, low pressure engagement with push notifications and check-ins unwanted.

Failure Response

On a bad day, users want the app to restart with no reminders, judgement, or impact warnings. A clean slate is more motivating than countless reminders.

Competition

Leaderboards and social comparison are counterproductive. This would stress the user out and cause them to abandon the app.

Social Privacy

Stigma around smoking or using nicotine created a need for discreet app presentation with neutral branding and optional privacy modes.

Graph Clarity

The projected nicotine increase in the chart caused confusion. The Goal Streak label was also unclear without context. Both needed tooltips or inline explanations.

Reflection

What Worked Well

  • Device recognition: Users immediately understood what the physical device was and could map its affordances (buttons, mouthpiece, screen) onto existing mental models from conventional vapes.
  • Onboarding flow: The onboarding process was easy and intuitive, the task was completed without confusion.
  • Core app comprehension: After 30 seconds on the home screen the user was able to accurately describe the app’s purpose. Users also understood and believed in the reduction mechanism, seeing NIX as a genuine tool for nicotine reduction.

Areas for Improvement

  • Device thickness: The most consistent physical feedback was that the device is too thick. Future iterations should prioritize a flatter, slimmer profile.
  • Button placement: Button was too high on the device body preventing natural grip.
  • Graph and streak counter confusion: Many things in the digital prototype were not labeled or had no explanation. A help button or tooltip would be useful.
  • App engagement: Users may disengage with the app over time while still using the device. The app needs a low-friction, high-value reason to open.
  • Social privacy: The app’s current branding and home screen may feel too explicit for users who want to keep their nicotine use private. A discreet mode or neutral lock screen preview would be beneficial.

Next Steps

  • Revise physical device dimensions: Slimming down the width and reposition the button to accommodate natural grip.
  • Add a help button or tooltips: Adding a way for users who are confused with how to use the interface receive help or find an explanation.
  • Introduce a privacy/stealth mode: Allow users to set a neutral app icon and remove identifying content from the lock screen notification preview.
  • Design a passive engagement layer: Giving users a reason to open the app without requiring active input.
  • Run additional testing sessions: Broader testing across multiple user profiles (cigarette smokers, heavy vapers, quit-motivated vs. reduction-motivated users) is needed for future iterations or design decisions.
